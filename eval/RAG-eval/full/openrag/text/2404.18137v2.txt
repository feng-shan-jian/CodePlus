The role of substitution elasticity in Domar aggregation
Satoshi Nakano · Kazuhiko Nishimura
February 25, 2025
Abstract The economy-wide production network, i.e., the monetary in put-output coeﬃ-
cients, will no longer be stable against the general equilib rium propagation of sectoral pro-
ductivity shocks, when the substitution elasticities of se ctoral production are nonneutral.
The associated network transformation hence renders nonli nearity in aggregating sectoral
productivity shocks into the economy’s output, i.e., Domar aggregation. In this study, we
demonstrate the possibility of production networks being t ransformed into a singular one.
We also explore the conditions under which the productivity shocks experience synergism
in Domar aggregation.

1 Introduction
Suppose all industrial sectors of the entire economy revealconstant returns to scale. Then, for
each sector, the unit cost function with pregiven level of productivity becomes scale-free and
only dependent on factor prices. Owing to the fact that all commodities are made by means of
commodities, while assuming all commodities are produced b y diﬀerent but unique sectors
(i.e., no joint production) and that there is only one kind of primary factor not made by means
of commodities, the prices of commodities relative to the primary factor are determined as the
solution to the general equilibrium system of equations tha t equilibrate commodity prices to
unit costs of sectors. Shephard’s lemma nonetheless indica tes that input-output coeﬃcients
will only be aﬀected by the equilibrium price change, which w ill only be triggered by the
propagation of productivity shocks across all sectors. 1
The idea of aggregating productivity growths associated wi th industrial sectors into na-
tional aggregate output growth measures is called the Domar aggregation. In a closed econ-
omy, aggregate output growth in the above setting essentially equals the aggregated commod-
ity prices via the representative household’s indirect uti lity, where the commodity prices are
determined by the sectoral unit cost functions with product ivity growths exogenously given.
Hulten (1978) claimed that sectoral productivity growths c an be mapped into national ag-
gregate output growth by linear aggregation using Domar wei ghts. Linear aggregation of
sectoral productivity growths, however, is only possible i n an economy where the represen-
tative household and all the production sectors aggregate c ommodities in a Cobb-Douglas
fashion, so that the monetary input-output coeﬃcients rema in constant.2
Accordingly, Acemoglu et al (2012, 2017); Acemoglu and Azar (2020) studied Domar
aggregation under Cobb-Douglas technology where Domar wei ghts are the product of rep-
resentative household’s expenditure shares and the (monetary) Leontief inverse. In this case,
the volatility of the aggregate output ﬂuctuations will con tract disproportionally to the num-
ber of sectors whose productivity shocks are being aggregated, owing to the granular property
(Gabaix, 2011) of the Leontief inverse. Baqaee and Farhi (20 19); Nakano and Nishimura
(2021) then studied aggregate ﬂuctuations of an economy com prising non-Cobb-Douglas
technology and showed that aggregate ﬂuctuations will have nonnormal tails even if sectoral
productivity shocks have normal tails, owing to the nonline ar nature of Domar aggregation
that can potentially induce network transformation.
From empirical perspective, Kim et al (2017); Nakano and Nis himura (2024) examined
economy-wide propagation of innovation, where innovationis proxied by productivity shocks
and their propagation is concerned across the production network of sectors with empirically
estimated CES (constant elasticity of substitution) unit cost functions. In this setting, the ﬁxed
point solution to the general equilibrium system of equatio ns is solved by way of recursive
mapping from factor prices onto commodity unit costs which w ill globally converge to a
unique equilibrium, owing to the concavity of the mapping, i f only the solution existed in
the positive-ﬁnite domain. During the course of analyses, h owever, there were cases (of pro-
ductivity shocks) where the recursive mapping experienced nonconvergence, indicating the
absence of the solution in the positive-ﬁnite domain.

productivity shocks) and potential zero/inﬁnite prices (w hich thus implies singularity of the

5 Concluding Remarks
According to Eden et al (2012), singularity hypotheses refe r to two distinct scenarios. One
is the intelligence explosion (Good, 1966), whereby machin e intelligence surpasses human
intelligence through computational advancement, which entails recursive self-improvement.
The other scenario is the biointelligence explosion, which entails the ampliﬁcation of human
capabilities to overcome all existing human limitations (Kurzweil, 2005). In both ways, how-
ever, the concept of singularity (i.e., explosion) does not go beyond exponential growth, if
not extreme. The economic singularity theory of Nordhaus (2 021) also exploits the idea of
extreme exponential growth of intelligence capital deepening by a large elasticity of substitu-
tion. In contrast, the singularity presented in this study i s based on matrix singularity where
the matrix is transformed by virtue of the substitution elas ticity that is nonneutral.
Of course, positive singularity cannot take place unless so me ﬁnite amount of non-labor
commodity can completely substitute the primary factor of p roduction. Our model allowed
convergence towards positive singularity because our (sta ndard) CES production function
allowed production even at the extreme position of the isoqu ant near the axes. On the other
hand, negative singularity is not an improbable event. With some productivity-diminishing
innovations, such as those involving environmental conservation technologies, network trans-
formation under inelastic network can encounter a state tha t some commodity cannot be
produced in net. Finally, as regards the matter of industria l policy, whether to promote a
portfolio of projects, or rather to take a selective policy, depends on the synergism inherent
in the production network where the elasticity of substitut ion plays the important role.
